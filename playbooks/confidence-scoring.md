# Confidence scoring

Score each claim or relationship, not the number of tools that repeat it. Record the confidence value and a short rationale alongside the finding.

Assess:

- source reliability and information credibility;
- directness of the observation and quality of the derivation;
- freshness and whether the observation is current or historical;
- independence of corroboration;
- consistency with other evidence;
- contradictions, alternative explanations, and unresolved gaps.

Use these bands consistently:

- **high** — strong, direct, recent evidence with credible independent corroboration and no material contradiction;
- **medium** — credible but limited, indirect, stale, partially corroborated, or with a manageable contradiction;
- **low** — weak, ambiguous, single-source, stale, unverified, or materially contradicted lead.

Do not upgrade confidence because multiple tools use the same upstream dataset. Do not infer ownership or identity from plausibility, absence of contrary evidence, shared infrastructure, or username reuse alone. When evidence is insufficient, preserve the hypothesis or unknown label and state what collection would resolve it. A source with no usable evidence is recorded exactly as `no data found`.
